  > and let users choose between them. I suggest use of a small panel of AI judges for the early

## Goal

Each model, and later the corpus, split into a few **subjects**: groups of diagrams (and the
elements they show) that a person would call natural, each mostly independent of the others.
Each subject gets a label. The splits are precomputed. One split serves three uses:
- **browsing** with no query: model, then subject;
- **grouping a query's results** by subject: "12 subjects, best match of each, 23 more like
  this", in place of a flat list of hundreds;
- **a diverse first page:** the top results drawn from different subjects.

- **Versions** (the maintainer, approving the plan: "Make sure you also test cases where some
  models are different 'versions' of another that mostly share items. The ability to isolate and
  group those is a low hanging fruit for splitting that can also serve as a baseline."):
  - a corpus is first split into **version families**, models that share most of their element
    ids (ADR-0020's groups);
  - within a family, a diagram present in several versions (the same id) is one item, shown once
    with its versions, so grouped results aren't flooded with copies;
  - every model-level split is then judged on families, not on copies.

## What we know, and don't

- **The data** is in the catalog (`index/catalog.jsonl`): each element lists the diagrams that
  show it, and each diagram its package and kind. The reference tree (`out/v020/on`, samples and
  fiction) has 3,645 diagrams and 1,761 packages in 27 projects. Element ids are shared across
  versions of a model, so versions can fall in the same subject.
- **The about text** (ADR-0029) says what each package and diagram is for, without names, and
  classes it (`about_class`). It is a ready description of subject matter.
- **No ground truth:** the fictional projects are organized by kind (requirements, structure),
  not by subject, and the maintainer's models can't be seen. Hence the judges.
- **A risk:** an element on many diagrams (the system block, say) can tie everything into one
  group. The remedy, discounting elements by how many diagrams show them, is a guess until
  measured.

## Candidate splits

| Id | Split | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| V | Version families: models joined by shared element ids, and a diagram's copies in the family joined into one item. A corpus-level split, and the first step of every other | none |
| R | Random, into as many groups as G: the floor that the judges must see through | none |
| P | The package tree, cut where it gives 4 to 15 groups: factual, what Cameo shows | none |
| G | Communities of diagrams, weighted by the elements they share (each discounted by how many diagrams show it) and the relationships between them; Louvain, seeded, as for modules (ADR-0013) | none |
| T | Clusters of words: diagram names, the names on them, package paths, the about text; TF-IDF | none |
| L1 | The LLM names and adjusts G: G's groups, with members' names and about text, go to the model, which may merge or split them, and names each | a request per group, about |
| L2 | The LLM proposes splits: the package tree with counts, and the diagrams' names and about classes, go to the model, which proposes 2 or 3 viable splits and assigns every diagram | a few requests per model; large models in parts |

The proposer is the tree's text model (gemma-4 here), as for users. The judges are other models,
so that no model judges its own work.

## How splits are judged

The panel: DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B, and gpt-oss-120b (`docs/design/evaluation.md`). Every item
shown to a judge carries its name, its package path and its about text.
- **E1, intruder:** five members of one group and one from another group of the same model,
  shuffled. "Which doesn't belong?" Chance is 1 in 6. It measures coherence and separation, and it
  is objective: the intruder is known.
- **E2, label fit:** one diagram, and the split's labels, shuffled. "Which subject is it in?"
  Agreement with the split measures its labels and its separation (L1, L2 and labelled P, G, T).
- **E3, preference:** two splits of the same model, as labels, sizes and three examples a group;
  each pair is asked in both orders. "Which would a systems engineer find more natural for
  discovering what this model covers?" Ties are allowed.
- **Measured without judges:**
  - the number of groups, and their balance (entropy, and the largest group's share);
  - singletons;
  - whether versions stay together: a diagram's copies across versions in the same group;
  - for V: the families found, against the known ones (below), as precision and recall of pairs
    of models.

**The version cases:**
- **Real:** TMT and TMT-2024x (98% of ids shared), the only pair among the samples.
- **Synthetic,** derived from fictional projects by `evaluation.fiction.versions`, as a separate
  set (the fiction and its questions are unchanged, so retrieval baselines don't move):
  - a later version (about 90% shared: elements and a diagram added, some renamed, some moved);
  - a fork (about 60% shared: a new package of its own, many renamed);
  - a copy saved again unchanged (100%), as Cameo users do.
- **Rivals, which aren't versions:** Riverbend's three proposals, same subject and same file
  name, no shared ids. V must keep them apart; a subject split across models may rightly join
  them (SB-08).
- **Agreement:** kappa between the judges, for each test. The lower-grade rule applies where
  grades are combined (ADR-0022).

## Offering more than one split

The maintainer suggests letting users choose between splits. ADR-0027 says heuristics are
measured defaults, not settings. A way to honor both:
- **No setting:** every tree has the same splits.
- **Views in the page,** chosen as one chooses a sort order:
  - the default subject split, from the judges;
  - the package tree, which is fact, not heuristic.
- **A second subject split** is offered only if the judges can't tell it from the default.

## Cost

- **Judges,** short text requests: E1 with 3 trials a group, E2 with 20 diagrams a model, and E3
  with all pairs, on 12 models and 6 splits. About 4,000 requests a judge, under $5 in all.
- **The proposer:** a few hundred requests on gemma-4, under $1.
- **Time:** about two hours of requests at concurrency 8, run as one capped job at a time.

## When to stop and ask

- **The judges can't see through R** (E1 within 10 points of the best split), or disagree at kappa
  under 0.2 on E1: the protocol is broken. Report before going on.
- **G collapses** (one group over half a model's diagrams, even with the discount): report, with
  what was tried.
- **Nothing beats P:** ship browsing by package, results grouped by package, and report.
- **Before CP3,** which changes the search page: the judged splits, and a page to look at.
