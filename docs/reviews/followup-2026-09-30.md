## Findings

### FU-001: Every directed diagram edge is listed and drawn in reverse

**Severity:** High · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/diagrams.py` (`edges`, `describe`, `render_png`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30. Every drawn edge that shows a relationship runs from source to target on the drone sample and on all of TMT's directed edges (a test invariant).

A diagram's connection list takes each edge's direction from the order of the drawn path's
ends (`linkFirstEndID`, `linkSecondEndID`). Cameo stores the target as the first end, so every
directed edge reads backwards. Those edges include control and object flows, dependencies,
«satisfy», «deriveReqt», generalizations, «include» and transitions. The sketch's arrowheads
are drawn at the wrong end for the same reason.

These lists appear on every diagram page, and the LLM prompt presents them as the
authoritative description of the diagram. The model then either repeats a reversed direction
or quietly overrides it, and neither outcome can be trusted. `relationships.csv`, the element
sections and the ledgers take their directions from the model and are correct.

**Evidence:**
- Across every drawn edge that shows a model relationship, the drawn direction is the reverse
  of the model's source and target:

  | Sample | Reversed edges |
  |---|---|
  | Package_Delivery_Drone | all of them: 41 Abstraction, 34 ControlFlow, 53 ObjectFlow, 2 Include |
  | TMT | all but one of 5,554 (the exception is a single Transition): 3,309 ControlFlow, 918 ObjectFlow, 392 Generalization, 339 Dependency, 241 Transition, 182 InterfaceRealization, 126 Usage, 22 Include, 22 Abstraction, 2 InformationFlow |

- **Example:** the drone's use-case page lists `Monitor Power →[Include]→ Deliver Package`,
  but the model's `<include>` elements are owned by Deliver Package, with Monitor Power as the
  `addition`.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-001R1 (done):** When an edge shows a model relationship, orient it by the relationship's source
  and target. Otherwise, take `linkSecondEndID` as the source, which matches every measured
  case.
- **FU-001R2 (done):** Draw the arrowhead at the target end of the path.
- **FU-001R3 (done):** Add a fixture layout that stores an «include» and a «satisfy» edge in Cameo's
  order. Add an invariant for every sample run: each directed edge with a relationship is
  listed as source → target, as in `relationships.csv`.

### FU-002: Item flows on connectors are missing from diagram text

**Severity:** High · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/diagrams.py:describe`, `src/cameo_ingest/semantics.py`

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30. R2 needed no change: item flows name blocks, and block sections already list them (138 lines in the drone sample).

In internal block diagrams, what each connector carries is modelled as an «ItemFlow» (an
InformationFlow with `realizingConnector` pointing at the connector and `conveyed` pointing at
the items). The connection list names only the connector itself, so the most informative part
of the diagram ("Energy", "Throttle Control Signal", "Drone Arrival Alert", "Forces") is
missing. Pairs of parts joined by two connectors show up as identical duplicate lines. The
labels are visible in the sketch, but the model, told that the list is authoritative, left
them out.

**Evidence:**
- **Diagram page:** the drone's `Drone` diagram lists 18 bare `—[Connector]—` lines, while its
  sketch shows about 20 item-flow labels.
- **The model:** it has 82 `realizingConnector` references and 91 «ItemFlow» stereotype
  applications.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-002R1 (done):** Index information flows by `realizingConnector` (and by `realization`, for
  flows realized by associations). Show their conveyed items on the connector edge, for
  example `—[Connector: Energy; Charging Complete Alert]—`, both in the diagram page and in
  the prompt.
- **FU-002R2 (no change needed):** Check where item flows appear in the sections of the parts and blocks at each
  end, and list them under the owning block where they don't. Parts are members, not
  sections, so today these flows may not appear anywhere readable.
- **FU-002R3 (done):** Add a fixture connector realized by an item flow, and assert that the diagram
  page shows the conveyed items.

### FU-003: Diagram labels: unnamed pins read `: Type`, and sketches cut off types and show «DiagramInfo»

**Severity:** Low · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/diagrams.py` (`element_label`, `render_png`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30.

- **Unnamed pins and parts:** these are labelled with an empty name followed by the type, as
  in `: Low Battery Alert →[ObjectFlow]→ Monitor Power.output : Low Battery Alert` in the
  activity diagram.
- **Cut-off labels:** sketch labels wrap to at most three lines, and the stereotype comes
  first, so the type is what gets cut off (`«PartProperty» communication Subsystem :`).
- **The frame's stereotype:** a diagram frame shows `«DiagramInfo»` as if it were a
  stereotype, because it now is one (BASE-001).

All three make both the page and the prompt harder to read.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-003R1 (done):** Label an unnamed typed element by its type alone, without the leading colon.
- **FU-003R2 (done):** Leave `DiagramInfo` out of the stereotypes shown on diagram frames.
- **FU-003R3 (done):** When a sketch box is small, keep the name and type in preference to the
  stereotype.

### FU-004: Diagram descriptions invent groupings and use Markdown formatting

**Severity:** Medium · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/pipeline.py` (`DIAGRAM_PROMPT`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30 by `diagram-description@v2`: in spot check 3, no invented groupings and 13 of 13 answers in plain prose.

- **Invented grouping:** the activity diagram's description says its actions are "categorized
  by function" and names four groups ("Power & Maintenance", "Logistics"...). The diagram has
  no such grouping, so this structure is presented as the model's own content when it isn't.
- **Formatting:** it uses bold pseudo-headings, bullet lists and backticked names. The summary
  prompt asks for plain prose, and the diagram prompt doesn't. Markup is noise in chunk text,
  and it makes descriptions inconsistent with each other.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-004R1 (done):** Revise `DIAGRAM_PROMPT`:
  - ask for plain prose, with no headings, bold text or code formatting;
  - group or categorize only what the diagram itself groups (frames, partitions, nesting);
  - explain the connection notation (source → target, with the relationship kind and any
    conveyed items in brackets).
- **FU-004R2 (done):** Compare the same items before and after the change, with the tooling of the
  quality plan (FU-006).

### FU-005: Package summaries see only the first 12,000 characters of a package

  Compare the options with the quality tooling before choosing.

### FU-007: Sketches are cluttered with small squares and lack clear arrows

**Severity:** Medium · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Reported by:** David Barbour · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/diagrams.py` (`render_png`, `_arrow`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30. Connector ends and roles are no longer drawn; pins and ports are dots; arrowheads sit at the target, in each kind's notation; item flows get a mid-line arrow.

The sketch draws every presentation element that has a rectangle as a box. That includes
decorations: connector ends and role markers, which Cameo stores as small views of their
own, and pins. The result is rows of small, meaningless squares along the edges of shapes.
Arrowheads are 9 px wide, often too small to see once the image is scaled down (FU-012), and
they sit at the wrong end (FU-001). Undirected kinds, such as connectors carrying directed
item flows, have none.

**Evidence:** the drone sample's 9 sketches contain 146 small boxes of 20 px or less: 62 are
`ConnectorEnd`, 26 `Role`, 58 `Pin` and 12 `FlowConnector`. See the `Drone` internal block
diagram in spot check 1.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-007R1 (done):** Don't draw connector ends and role markers. Draw pins and ports as small
  markers on their owner's border, not as boxes.
- **FU-007R2 (done):** Draw arrowheads at the target end (FU-001), sized for the final image. Use the
  notation of each kind: a hollow triangle for generalization and realization, an open arrow
  otherwise, and dashed lines for dependencies. Show the direction of item flows on the
  connectors that carry them (FU-002).

### FU-008: Wrapped labels make sketches hard to read; a legend would serve better

**Severity:** Medium · **Verified:** By inspection · **Reported by:** David Barbour · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/diagrams.py` (`render_png`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30. Shapes are numbered in the sketch and in the page's legend; names are drawn on one line when they fit, cut with an ellipsis when not, and never wrapped.

Labels are wrapped to the width of their box and cut to three lines, so long names and types
end up broken or missing. The stereotype comes first, so it is usually the type that gets
cut. The maintainer's proposal is a legend: short tags in the sketch, and the full labels in
text next to it.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-008R1 (done):** Tag each shape in the sketch with a short number, and write the full label
  (kind, stereotype, name, type) in a text legend keyed by those numbers. A short name that
  fits may be drawn in full, next to its number.
- **FU-008R2 (done):** Make the legend the shape list in the prompt, so the model can map what it
  sees in the image to exact names. Connections then refer to the same numbers.

### FU-009: Diagram requests mix structure and meaning

**Severity:** Medium · **Verified:** By inspection · **Reported by:** David Barbour · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/prompts.py` (`diagram-description@v1`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30 by `diagram-description@v2`, which asks for meaning (spot check 3: diagram usefulness 3.6 → 4.25).

The request asks for the subject, the elements, their arrangement and the relationships all
at once, so most of every answer restates structure that the diagram page already lists
exactly. The meaning (what the diagram says about the system, and why the modeller drew it)
gets only a sentence or two. The maintainer's options: emphasize meaning, or ask two separate
questions, one about structure and one about meaning.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-009R1 (done):** Emphasize meaning. The structure is already extracted exactly on the
  diagram page, so `diagram-description@v2` asks what the diagram tells a reader about the
  system: purpose, behaviour, responsibilities, the key flows and their significance. It uses
  the legend and connections as evidence, and doesn't list them again. Two separate queries
  would double the cost for a structural answer we already have. Compare v1 and v2 with the
  quality tooling (FU-006).

### FU-012: The model sees images at 768 px; sketches are drawn at up to 2000 px

**Severity:** High · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Reported by:** David Barbour · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/diagrams.py` (`MAX_SIDE`), `src/cameo_ingest/pipeline.py`

**Status:** In progress: R1 and R3 are done (sketches at `--image-size`, embedded images scaled to it); R2 is plan DV.

`google/gemma-4-31B-it` on DeepInfra squeezes every image into 768 × 768 px. DeepInfra does not
offer, or does not honour, the API options for variable patch counts or pooling that would
keep more detail. Sketches are drawn at up to 2000 px on the longer side, so the model sees
them at about a third of their size, and labels drawn at 12 px become unreadable. This may
explain why the model sometimes ignored labels plainly drawn in the sketch. Embedded images
are sent at full size and shrunk the same way.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-012R1 (done; superseded by FU-015R1):** Draw sketches for the size the model actually sees: a setting (`--llm-image-size`,
  default 768), with fonts and arrowheads sized for it. The pages can keep a larger rendering
  for people.
- **FU-012R2:** When a diagram can't be drawn legibly at that size, split it (FU-011) rather
  than shrink it further. When it has to be shrunk anyway, say so in the request log's notes.
- **FU-012R3 (done):** Scale embedded images down to the same size before sending them, and record
  their original size.

### FU-013: The diagram template doesn't say which way dependency arrows point

**Severity:** Medium · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/prompts.py` (`diagram-description@v2`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30. See "As built" below.

Found in spot check 3. Now that edges are drawn correctly (FU-001), a «deriveReqt» arrow runs
from the derived requirement to its source, as SysML draws it. The template calls the ends
"source" and "target" without saying what that means for a dependency. The model read
`Avoid Collisions →[Abstraction: «DeriveReqt»]→ Maneuver Autonomously` as "Maneuver
Autonomously is derived from Avoid Collisions", which is backwards. The package summary,
working from element sections that say "derived from" in words, got the same relationships
right. The template's request to name the flows "that matter most" also drew generic filler
sentences.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-013R1 (done):** Write `diagram-description@v3`. It says that a dependency arrow runs from the
  dependent element to what it depends on:
  - X →«deriveReqt»→ Y: X is derived from Y;
  - X →«satisfy»→ R: X satisfies R;
  - A →Generalization→ B: A is a kind of B.

  It also asks what the main flows or dependencies achieve, instead of which "matter most".
- **FU-013R2 (done):** Check the requirement diagram again, along with a regression check of the
  other diagrams.

**As built:** stating the rule in `diagram-description@v3` was not enough. Gemma still read
`Avoid Collisions →«DeriveReqt»→ Maneuver Autonomously` backwards, twice. What worked was
putting the verb in each dependency line, so that it reads correctly left to right:
`Avoid Collisions →[Dependency: «DeriveReqt»; is derived from]→ Maneuver Autonomously`. The
verbs are `diagrams.VERBS`, and the wording matches the ledgers. The requirement diagram's
description now reads the derivations correctly ("Maneuver Autonomously is derived into …
Avoid Collisions and Execute Landing"). Only the 2 diagrams whose lines changed were asked
again. The lesson for templates: for this model, put meaning in the data rather than in rules
about notation.

### FU-014: A new prompt template version doesn't rewrite existing projects

**Severity:** Medium · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/cli.py` (options), `src/cameo_ingest/prompts.py`

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30.

Found while checking FU-013. Moving the pipeline to `diagram-description@v3` left `out/drone`
untouched ("0 project(s) written"). A written project is rewritten only when the tool version
or the options hash changes, and the prompt templates in use were in neither. So a project
kept answers from older templates indefinitely. The same holds for any change to the output
format made without a version bump.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-014R1 (done):** `prompts.CURRENT` names the template versions in use, and their keys are
  part of a run's options, so a project written with other versions is written again. This is
  tested by switching a template back and forth.
- **FU-014R2 (done):** The version is bumped to 0.2.0: the output has changed a great deal since
  0.1.0, and a version change is what tells existing trees to rewrite. It should be bumped
  whenever the output format changes.

### FU-015: Sketches don't use the model's pixel budget, and images come after the text

**Severity:** Medium · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Reported by:** David Barbour (research) · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/diagrams.py` (`render_png`), `src/cameo_ingest/llm.py` (`ask`), `src/cameo_ingest/pipeline.py` (`_fit_image`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30. The drone sample's sketches now use 580,000 to 636,000 of the 645,120 pixels (a wide one used about 355,000 before); the one-shape profile diagram stays small, since enlargement is capped at 2x.

`docs/research/gemma4-images-2026-09-30.md` corrects FU-012's premise. Gemma-4 does not squeeze
images into 768 × 768 px:
- **Budget:** it has 280 soft tokens per image on DeepInfra, which ignores requests for more.
- **Pixels:** that comes to at most 280 × 48² = 645,120 pixels.
- **Scaling:** it scales each image to fill that area at the image's own aspect ratio, with
  sides in multiples of 48.

FU-012R1 drew sketches at 768 px on the longer side, so a typical wide sketch uses only part of
the budget. For example, 768 × 462 is 355,000 pixels, and the model enlarges it, gaining
nothing. A 2:1 sketch could be drawn at 1104 × 576 for the same tokens. The research also
passes on Google's advice to put images before the text in a request. Ours come after it.

**Remediation:**
- **FU-015R1 (done):** Draw sketches to fill a pixel budget (`--image-pixels`, default 645,120, which is
  gemma-4's 280 tokens) at the diagram's own aspect ratio, with sides in multiples of 48.
  Scale embedded images the same way. This replaces `--image-size`.
- **FU-015R2 (done):** Put the image before the text in vision requests, as new template versions
  (`diagram-description@v4`, `image-description@v2`).
- **FU-015R3 (done):** Update the slot descriptions and the README, and re-record the replay fixture
  (its image requests change).

### FU-020: Hex-encoded images in tagged values fill pages and chunks

**Severity:** High · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/emit.py` (`tagged_values`, `table_config`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30.

Found while measuring packages for plan DV-05. SAF_FFDS stores 115 images (88 SVG, 27 PNG) in the
`Content` tag of «CustomImageHolder», as hex text ("3c 3f 78 6d 6c..."): 10.4 million characters

- **Pages:** a single line of 710,704 characters.
- **Chunks:** a chunk that size is far beyond any embedding model's input limit.
- **Summary inputs:** they made SAF_FFDS's "COTS Physical Systems" package 3.6 million
  characters, of which the summary saw the first 12,000.

  with their full length noted. That keeps every prose value seen in the samples. The CSV of
  tagged values and the element index keep full values.
- **FU-020R2 (done):** Every output tree is checked for chunk lines over 8,000 characters. A
  unit test covers the rule. SAF_FFDS's largest package input falls from 3.6 million

### FU-021: Relationship lists don't say which way a dependency reads

**Severity:** Medium · **Verified:** Reproduced · **Where:** `src/cameo_ingest/emit.py` (`section`)

**Status:** Fixed on 2026-09-30.

Found in the first part summaries of the drone's "Requirements Domain" (plan DV-05). An element
section lists its relationships with bare arrows: "DeriveReqt ← [Competitive Operating
Cost]" on the requirement that others are derived from. The model misread them:
- **Reversed:** "Provide Package Delivery is derived by several elements".
- **Wrong kind:** «DeriveReqt» links read as refinements ("is further refined by").

FU-013 fixed the same misreading in diagram text by writing the verb into each line; element

**Remediation:**

  flows, keep their arrows.

### FU-022: Summaries of analysis-result packages are lists of instance names
