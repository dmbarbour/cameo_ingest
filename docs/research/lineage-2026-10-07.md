# Research: telling versions from rivals by who made the ids, 2026-10-07

Plan LN (`docs/plans/lineage-2026-10-07.md`), CP1: whether a model's element ids, which carry a
maker and a creation time, tell a later version from a model derived by someone else, and from
rivals on a shared root (TR-003; the maintainer: "it's reasonably difficult to distinguish
versioned models vs. rival models with a shared root based on save time or shared content").

## The ids

Most Cameo ids read `_<tool version>_<hex id>_<creation time, epoch ms>_<random>_<counter>`, and
MagicDraw's older ones `eee_<epoch ms>_<random>_<counter>` (`fingerprint.parse_id`). The hex id
is taken for the maker. That it names a user, a machine or an installation is an inference from
the shape: no documentation was found. On the samples, 57% to 99% of a model's ids carry a maker
(TMT 80%, NIST 99%, OpenSUT_MPS 57%); single-author models show one maker, TMT 17. Others are
UUIDs and the like.

Cameo's project id doesn't help: TMT and TMT-2024x, versions, have different ones, and two SAF
templates, different models, share one.

## The cases

- **Synthetic** (`evaluation.fiction.lineage`): fictional projects with their ids rewritten in
  Cameo's shape, makers and times known: a later save; a customer's tender and two bids on it
  (one with a second version); an unchanged copy; two unrelated models that take in one library.
  9 pairs.
- **Real** (`scripts/fetch_lineage_cases.py`, from public git histories, found by a survey):
  - SAF's sample model, 6 versions, 2021 to 2026;
  - OpenSUT's RTS (4) and overview (2);
  - NIST's flow network across a rename (4);
  - TMT: master's 2019 model, a file from each of two branches made from it, and master's
    2023 model.

  16 pairs with a known relation, 34 related pairs in all. No real rivals were found: a public
  set of bids on one customer model doesn't seem to exist. The TMT `feature/omg-sst` branch's
  2023 file is the 2019 root saved again (2 ids differ), so the real cases hold no true branches
  either.

## The rules (`lineage.classify`)

For a pair sharing ADR-0020's "related" share or more, with the older first:

| Kind | When |
|---|---|
| copy | neither side has more than 0.1% (or 2) ids of its own |
| branches, root | each side has at least 3% (and 20) of its own, at least 60% of them made after the shared part's latest; branches when each side's own were made by the other's makers (60%), root (rivals) when not |
| version | the newer's own made after the shared part (60%), the older's own mostly before it, and at least 15% of the newer's own by the older's makers; or the newer has almost none of its own |
| derived | the same, with under 15% by the older's makers |
| related | otherwise: much of each side's own predates the shared part (a library) |
| unknown | under 30% of the deciding side's own ids carry a maker |

## Results

| Set | Right | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic | 9 of 9 | |
| Real, with a known relation | 15 of 16 | The miss: two RTS files committed the same day; the one saved first has 98 own ids, none with a maker, and neither side's own postdate the shared part: "related", the cautious answer |
| Real, the other 18 pairs | all versions across chains, as they should be | |

**What the first rules got wrong, and why** (8 of 16 real pairs right):
- **Teams change.** The first rules asked that 60% of a version's new ids be by the older's
  makers. Real versions show 21% (TMT), 31% to 51% (SAF): new people, or new installations. A
  bid on a customer's model shows 0%. The line is at 15%.
- **Rework shares little.** The first rules, like ADR-0020, needed a Jaccard of 0.5 (or 80% of
  the smaller) before calling a pair versions. SAF from 2021 to 2024 shares 0.31, TMT from 2019
  to 2023 0.32 to 0.39. Time tells better than share: a version's own ids come after the shared
  part (95% to 100%), the older's own, those dropped, mostly before (0% to 49%). A library taken
  into models made before it has the models' own mostly before it.
- **"Copy" was too loose** at 2% of a side: real small revisions (SAF, 11 and 51 ids of 5,000)
  read as copies.

## What it means

- **Versions are told well,** across rework and changes of team, on 4 real chains.
- **Models derived by others, and rivals on a shared root,** are told on synthetic cases only.
  Their signature (new work by makers the other side lacks, after the shared part) is clean in
  construction; the maintainer's trial is the test on real bids.
- **ADR-0020's version rule misses real versions after rework,** and would join rival bids
  (they share 77% to 79%, Jaccard). Plan LN's CP2 makes families from lineage instead.

## Caveats

- Thresholds were set on these cases: the real ones are 4 chains, all by small teams.
- Whether a hex id stays with a person across machines, or with a machine across people, is
  unknown; either reading serves, as long as two organizations don't share one.
- A rival who edits the customer's root heavily, or a version made by an entirely new team,
  would read as something else: the evidence is shown with each kind for that reason.
