# Research: splitting models into subjects, judged by a panel, 2026-10-07

Plan SB (`docs/plans/subjects-2026-10-06.md`), CP1 and CP2: how to split a model's diagrams into
subjects for discovery (TR-002), measured on the public samples and the fiction by a panel of AI
judges, since the maintainer's models and queries can't be shared.

## Setup

- **The study tree** (`out/sb/tree`): the samples, the fiction, and three synthetic versions of
  fictional projects (`evaluation.fiction.versions`): a later save of Kestrel (91% of ids
  shared), a fork of Port Calder as Eastport (60%), a copy of Ashgrove (100%). 30 projects, 26
  families.
- **Families (V):** versions of a model, by ADR-0020's groups; each diagram taken once.
- **Splits,** into about the square root of n/2 groups (2 to 15):
  - R random, P the package tree, G communities of diagrams by shared elements and their owners,
    T word clusters (none needs the LLM);
  - L1, G's groups labelled by the tree's text model (gemma-4), alike merged;
  - L2a, L2b, L2c: three ways the model proposed from an outline of the model, each on its own
    principle, every diagram assigned by the model (30 a request).
- **The panel:** DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B, gpt-oss-120b, on 12 families of 10 to 1,349
  diagrams. Diagrams shown by name, owner, kind and about text, never package path.
- **Tests:**
  - E1, intruder: the one of six from another group (chance 17%);
  - E2, label fit: the subject a diagram belongs to, by the labels, chance-corrected; labels made
    without the diagrams asked about;
  - E3, preference: two splits, shown as labels, sizes and examples; counted only when a judge
    answers the same in both orders.

## Results

**Version families:** all four found (TMT and TMT-2024x, and the three synthetic ones), and
Riverbend's three rival proposals kept apart. Taking each diagram once halves TMT, from 2,689
copies to 1,349 diagrams.

| Split | E1 intruder | E2 label fit | E3: verdicts won, of those held in both orders |
|---|---|---|---|
| R | 15% | -0.02 | lost to P, G, T about 3 to 1 (CP1) |
| P | 59% (7 families) | 0.25 | even with G; lost every one to L1 and L2 |
| G | 56% | 0.30 | even with P; lost every one to L1 and L2 |
| T | 53% | 0.24 | even with P and G (CP1) |
| L1 | 50% | 0.39 | beat P and G 36 to 0; lost to L2a, b and c, 22 to 43 in all |
| L2a, by engineering activity | 57% | 0.54 | beat P and G |
| L2b, by part of the system | 53% | 0.58 | beat P and G |
| L2c, by kind of concern | 58% | 0.56 | beat P and G |

Differences, with a bootstrap over families (95%):
- **E1:** no LLM split differs significantly from G. L1 is a little worse (−0.05 [−0.10,
  −0.03]): merging the groups it names alike makes looser groups.
- **E2:** L2a, L2b and L2c beat G by +0.28 to +0.33, each significant. L1 beats G by +0.14
  [+0.00, +0.30].
- **E3:** the ways L2a, L2b and L2c can't be told apart (most verdicts change with the order).

**Agreement:** the judges agree at kappa 0.62 to 0.72 on E1 and E2. On E3, two of them prefer
the split shown first (DeepSeek 60%, Qwen 56% here; 76% in CP1, when the splits shown were
closer). Only verdicts given in both orders count.

## What it means

- **Without the LLM, nothing beats anything:** P, G and T group about equally well. P can't split
  5 of the 12 families (all their diagrams in one package); G always splits.
- **The LLM's groups aren't more coherent, but they can be named and used.** E1 is flat. L1
  (G's groups, labelled) beats G 36 to 0 on E3 with the same groups, so E3 mostly judges the
  labels. L2 beats L1 on E3 and E2, so proposing the subjects first, then assigning, does better
  than naming groups found otherwise.
- **E2 favors L2 by construction:** L2's groups are what a model assigned by the labels, and E2
  asks other models to do the same. The judges agreeing with gemma-4 says the labels are clear
  and the assignment consistent, not that the subjects are natural. E3 is the test of that, and
  it says so only through labels and examples.
- **The three ways are equally good, and different:** by engineering activity, by part of the
  system, by kind of concern. That fits the maintainer's idea of offering a few splits for users
  to choose between.
- **What only a person can say:** whether L2's subjects feel natural on real models. The
  maintainer's trial is the test.

## Caveats

- 12 families, of which 4 are larger than 100 diagrams, and only one (TMT) is large.
- The about texts come from gemma-4, as do L1 and L2: the panel judges text one model wrote.
- One run of each proposal, at temperature 0; another run could propose other ways.
